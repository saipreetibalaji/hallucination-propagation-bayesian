# Sequential Bayesian Modelling of Hallucination Propagation and Uncertainty in Large Language Models

> A research project investigating how incorrect, correct, and uncertain responses evolve across multi-step reasoning chains using first-order Markov chains and Bayesian transition modelling.

## Overview 

Large language models (LLMs) often solve multi-hop questions through a sequence of intermediate reasoning steps. An incorrect intermediate answer may influence later responses, while uncertainty or abstention may also emerge as a chain progresses.

This project studies these sequential response patterns using a Bayesian framework. It analyses responses generated by **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct** on multi-hop questions from the **MuSiQue** dataset and models transitions between three operational response states:

- **C — Correct:** response classified as correct by the project's automatic answer-matching procedure.
- **H — Incorrect / hallucination proxy:** response classified as incorrect and non-abstaining.
- **U — Uncertain / abstention proxy:** response detected as hedging or abstaining.

The project compares two generation conditions:

1. **Oracle-assisted independent reasoning:** later sub-questions are supplied with gold intermediate answers.
2. **Self-conditioned dependent reasoning:** later sub-questions use the model's previous answers and preceding question-answer context.

A first-order Markov model is fitted separately for each condition. Dirichlet priors provide Bayesian posterior distributions over transition probabilities, allowing posterior means and credible intervals to be estimated.

## Research questions

1. How do response states change across consecutive reasoning steps?
2. Does a response classified as incorrect tend to be followed by another incorrect response, an uncertain response, or a correct response?
3. How do transition patterns differ between oracle-assisted independent reasoning and self-conditioned dependent reasoning?
4. How well do training-derived transition probabilities predict response states on validation and held-out internal test partitions?

## Methodology

### 1. Sequential response-state construction

Each generated answer is mapped to one of the operational states C, H, or U using automatic heuristics. These labels make the sequential analysis possible, but they are not equivalent to human annotation:

- C is a heuristic correctness label.
- H is an incorrect, non-abstaining response proxy; it is not necessarily a human-verified hallucination.
- U is a hedge/abstention proxy; it is not a calibrated measure of epistemic uncertainty.

No manual validation of these labels is claimed in this project.

### 2. Question-level data partitioning

The 300 unique question IDs are split into:

| Partition | Questions | Proportion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Training | 210 | 70% |
| Validation | 45 | 15% |
| Test | 45 | 15% |

The split is stratified by chain length. Both condition records belonging to the same question are kept in the same partition to prevent question-level leakage.

This is a **retrospective internal holdout**, not the official MuSiQue test set. The question sample had already been generated and inspected before this split was introduced.

### 3. First-order Markov model

Let the response state at step \(t\) be \(X_t \in \{C,H,U\}\). The first-order Markov assumption is:

\[
P(X_{t+1}=j \mid X_t=i, X_{t-1},\ldots,X_1)
=
P(X_{t+1}=j \mid X_t=i).
\]

For each experimental condition, define a transition matrix:

\[
\mathbf{P} =
\begin{bmatrix}
P(C\mid C) & P(H\mid C) & P(U\mid C)\\
P(C\mid H) & P(H\mid H) & P(U\mid H)\\
P(C\mid U) & P(H\mid U) & P(U\mid U)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Rows represent the current state and columns represent the next state. Each row sums to one.

The primary Bayesian analysis estimates transitions from steps **1→2 and 2→3**. The 3→4 position is examined diagnostically because only four-step chains contribute to it and the homogeneous-transition assumption may not hold across all positions.

### 4. Bayesian transition estimation

For each current-state row \(i\), let the transition counts be:

\[
(n_{iC}, n_{iH}, n_{iU}).
\]

A symmetric Dirichlet prior is used:

\[
(\theta_{iC},\theta_{iH},\theta_{iU})
\sim \operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,1,1).
\]

Given observed transition counts, the conjugate posterior is:

\[
(\theta_{iC},\theta_{iH},\theta_{iU})\mid D
\sim
\operatorname{Dirichlet}
(1+n_{iC},1+n_{iH},1+n_{iU}).
\]

The posterior mean for a transition probability is:

\[
E[\theta_{ij}\mid D]
=
\frac{1+n_{ij}}{3+n_{iC}+n_{iH}+n_{iU}}.
\]

Posterior samples are used to calculate 95% credible intervals and differences between the two conditions. The validation and test partitions are evaluated using the transition estimates learned from the training partition; they are not used to fit the primary posterior.

### 5. Predictive evaluation

The notebook reports:

- **Multiclass Brier score:** lower is better.
- **Multiclass log loss:** lower is better.
- **Argmax accuracy:** higher is better.

These metrics assess next-state prediction. They do not by themselves establish that one prompting condition causes fewer hallucinations.

## Main findings

The revised run's training-only posterior estimates include:

| Quantity | Independent | Dependent |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(P(H_{t+1}\mid H_t)\) | 0.293 | 0.267 |
| \(P(X_{t+1}\ne C\mid H_t)\) | 0.366 | 0.750 |
| \(P(H_{t+1}\mid C_t)\) | 0.118 | 0.226 |

For the difference in the probability of remaining non-correct after H (dependent minus independent), the estimated difference is **0.384**, with a 95% credible interval of **[0.193, 0.560]**.

The key interpretation is that the self-conditioned dependent condition has a higher estimated probability of remaining in a non-correct state after an H state. This does **not** mean that direct H→H repetition is higher: the estimated H→H probabilities are 0.293 (independent) and 0.267 (dependent), and the credible interval for their difference includes zero.

Validation and test performance are not uniformly better for one condition. The results should be interpreted as predictive evaluation on the particular internal partitions, not as a universal ranking of the conditions.

## Limitations and responsible interpretation

1. **Heuristic state labels:** C/H/U are automatic operational labels, not human-validated annotations. H is a hallucination proxy, not a verified hallucination label. U is not calibrated uncertainty.
2. **Condition confounding:** the independent condition uses gold intermediate answers, whereas the dependent condition uses model-generated previous answers and context. The comparison does not isolate conversation history alone.
3. **Retrospective split:** the internal train/validation/test split was created after the original sample had already been generated and inspected. It is not an untouched benchmark test set.
4. **Markov homogeneity:** a single transition matrix assumes transition behaviour is stable across positions. The notebook includes a position diagnostic; later-step deviations should be discussed.
5. **Sample size:** the held-out partitions contain 45 questions each, so evaluation estimates have sampling uncertainty.
6. **Token scores:** generation-time token probabilities, if present in the source records, should not be interpreted as calibrated factual confidence unless separately validated.
